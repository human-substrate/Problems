## Series

### Alcohol Consumption per Capita (`alcoholPerCapita`)

- **Publisher:** NIAAA Surveillance Report #122, Table 1
- **Where:** <https://www.niaaa.nih.gov/sites/default/files/surveillance-report122.Per-Capita-Consumption.pdf> · <https://www.niaaa.nih.gov/publications/surveillance-reports>
- **Unit:** gallons of pure ethanol per person aged 14+, per year
- **Coverage:** 1850–2023
- **Method:** pdftotext -layout, Table 1 rows, 'All beverages' column
- **Note:** Apparent consumption: beverage sales and tax data converted to gallons of pure ethanol (beer + wine + spirits) divided by the population aged 14 and older (15 and older before 1970). NIAAA's Alcohol Epidemiologic Data System; a new surveillance report each spring with a ~16-month lag.

### Antidepressant Use in the Past Month (NHANES) (`antidepressantUse`)

- **Publisher:** NCHS, NHANES, Data Briefs 283 and 377
- **Where:** <https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/data/databriefs/db377-tables-508.pdf#4> · <https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/data/databriefs/db283.pdf>
- **Unit:** percent of persons, age population changes
- **Coverage:** 2002–2018
- **Method:** db283 Figure 4, PDF page 4, TSV chart coordinates, middle of three sex-specific labels per early cycle; db377 Figure 4 companion data table, Both sexes intersections by TSV coordinates
- **Note:** Sparse survey cycles keyed to cycle END year, a named exception to usual annual density expectations; no interpolation. Seven points across approximately 20 years. The early 1999–2002 and 2003–2006 pools cover ages 12 and over; the 2009–2010 through 2017–2018 cycles cover ages 18 and over. This is a genuine population break at the 2006→2010 gap, not a directly comparable change. Later db283 pools are excluded because they overlap db377's cycles. The early PDF is SHA256-verified against data/cdc/READ.md. Antidepressant use alone does not distinguish treatment access from illness prevalence.

### Can Name All Three Branches (phone era) (`appcBranchesPhone`)

- **Publisher:** Annenberg Public Policy Center, Constitution Day Civics Survey
- **Where:** <https://www.annenbergpublicpolicycenter.org/political-communication/civics-knowledge-survey/> · <https://www.annenbergpublicpolicycenter.org/wp-content/uploads/Appendix_APPC_Civics_Sept_2022a.pdf>
- **Unit:** percent of adults naming all three branches (telephone survey)
- **Coverage:** 2006–2022
- **Method:** transcribed from the CIVI3/CIVI4 summary table, p.2 of APPC's September 2022 appendix PDF (read 2026-08-31)
- **Note:** Same question as appcBranchesWeb, asked by telephone (RDD/CATI, ~1,000–1,400 adults) from 2006 through 2022. The series ends in 2022 when APPC moved to its online panel; APPC's 2022 parallel run (47% phone vs 72% online) measured a ~25-point mode effect, which is why the eras are separate series. Not asked 2007–2010 or 2012.

### Can Name All Three Branches of Government (`appcBranchesWeb`)

- **Publisher:** Annenberg Public Policy Center, Constitution Day Civics Survey
- **Where:** <https://www.annenbergpublicpolicycenter.org/political-communication/civics-knowledge-survey/> · <https://www.annenbergpublicpolicycenter.org/wp-content/uploads/APPC_Con_Day_Topline_2023_final.pdf> · <https://www.annenbergpublicpolicycenter.org/wp-content/uploads/Constitution_Day_Topline_2025.pdf>
- **Unit:** percent of adults naming all three branches (online panel)
- **Coverage:** 2022–2025
- **Method:** transcribed from APPC topline PDFs (read 2026-08-31): 2023 topline CIVI4 table (2023 + 2022 online panel), 2025 topline 'Number of Branches Correctly Recalled' table (2024, 2025)
- **Note:** "Would you mind naming any of them, the three branches of government?" — percent of all respondents naming executive, legislative, and judicial, from APPC's annual Constitution Day survey (SSRS Opinion Panel, self-administered online, fielded each August–September). The survey moved from telephone to this online panel: APPC's own 2022 parallel run measured 72% online against 47% by phone (~25-point mode effect) and says the online years "will not be presented as part of a historical trend" with the phone years — so the phone era is a separate line (appcBranchesPhone) and the two are never joined. The 2022 value here is that parallel online reading.

### Consumer Bankruptcies (`bankruptcies`)

- **Publisher:** Federal Reserve Bank of New York, Household Debt and Credit Report (Consumer Credit Panel/Equifax)
- **Where:** <https://www.newyorkfed.org/microeconomics/hhdc>
- **Unit:** thousands of consumers with a new bankruptcy notation per year
- **Coverage:** 2003–2025
- **Method:** HHD_C_Report_2026Q2.xlsx sheet "Page 17 Data" column bankruptcy, annual sum of quarters (full years only)
- **Note:** Number of consumers with a new bankruptcy notation on their credit file, summed across the four quarters of each year, from the New York Fed Consumer Credit Panel. The spike and collapse around October 2005 is the BAPCPA bankruptcy-reform filing rush. Only full years are included. US Courts' Table F-2 filing counts are the administrative alternative; this panel series is used because it is one machine-readable file back to 2003.

Years with fewer than 200 weighted responses are dropped.

### Books Read per Year (`booksPerYear`)

- **Publisher:** Gallup
- **Where:** <https://news.gallup.com/poll/388541/americans-reading-fewer-books-past.aspx>
- **Unit:** mean books read, all or part of the way through, past year
- **Coverage:** 1990–2021
- **Method:** transcribed from the trend prose and chart on Gallup poll page 388541 (read 2026-08-31): 1990 15.3, 1999 18.5, 2001 14.5, 2002 15.8, 2005 14.2, 2016 15.6, 2021 12.6
- **Note:** "How many books did you read, either all or part of the way through, in the past year?" — interviewers count printed books, e-books, and audiobooks. Gallup's trend, asked seven times between 1990 and 2021 (an irregular cadence, shown honestly). LAST ASKED December 2021: the series is dormant until Gallup re-asks. The 2021 reading (12.6) was the lowest Gallup had measured, driven by fewer heavy readers (27% read 10+ books, down 8 points from 2016).

### Frequent Mental Distress (BRFSS State Median) (`brfssFrequentMentalDistress`)

- **Publisher:** CDC, BRFSS Prevalence and Trends Data
- **Where:** <https://data.cdc.gov/resource/dttw-5yxu.json>
- **Unit:** percent of adults, median across states and DC
- **Coverage:** 2019–2024
- **Method:** Socrata Healthy Days / 14+ days when mental health not good; discover and validate median location, select Overall breakout, check distinct-year coverage
- **Note:** Mental health not good on 14 or more of the past 30 days. CDC's own published median across all states and DC (not a population-weighted national average; BRFSS does not publish one in this table). This resource starts in 2019, with six annual readings through 2024 in the verified edition, a named short-coverage exception. Pre-2019 coverage, including 2011–2018, needs annual XPT microdata and _LLCPWT weighting not currently read by this pipeline; those years are omitted, not interpolated.
